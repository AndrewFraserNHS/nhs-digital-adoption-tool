Guidance for Using the Stakeholder Analysis Tool

Introduction

Welcome to the Stakeholder Analysis Tool. This guide provides comprehensive instructions on how to use its features effectively. Stakeholder analysis is a critical process for identifying individuals or groups who can impact or are impacted by a project. This tool is designed to help you systematically analyse your stakeholders, devise engagement strategies, and track your progress.

Core Features: Managing Your Data

On the top-right of the Dashboard, you will find the main controls for managing your data.

  • Save to File: This is the most important button. It allows you to save your entire analysis (all stakeholders, engagement logs, and reference data) to a single .json file on your computer. When clicked, it will open a "Save As..." dialogue box, allowing you to choose where to save the file and what to name it. Good Practice: Save your work frequently and use descriptive filenames that include the date (e.g., Project-Alpha-Stakeholders-2025-10-12.json).
  • Load from File: This allows you to load a previously saved .json file, completely replacing any data currently in the tool. You will be asked to confirm this action as it cannot be undone.
  • Refresh: This button re-draws all the charts and tables on the screen, which can be useful if something doesn't appear to have updated correctly.
  • Clear Data: This will completely reset the tool to a blank state. You will be shown a warning and asked for confirmation, as this action is irreversible.

Step-by-Step Guide

Step 1: Customise Your Project (Dashboard Tab)

Before you begin, set up your project details on the Dashboard. You can click on the text for "Organisation" and "Project / Programme" to edit them. This information is saved with your file.

Step 2: Define Your Terms (Reference Data Tab)

The Reference Data tab is the best place to start. It allows you to customise the dropdown options used throughout the tool. This ensures consistency in your data entry.

  • How to use: Simply type your list of items into the text boxes, with one item per line. Click "Save" for that category when you are finished.
  • Good Practice: Define your Groups, Sub-Groups, Locations, and Relationships before adding any stakeholders. This makes the data entry process much smoother.

Step 3: Add Your Stakeholders (Stakeholders Tab)

This is the core of your analysis. Use the Add Stakeholder button to open the pop-up form and fill in the details.

  • Interest, Impact, Power, & Influence: These four ratings are the foundation of the analysis map. Assess each stakeholder from Low to Very High.
  • Commitment (Current vs. Target): Assess where the stakeholder is now (e.g., Resistant) and where you need them to be (e.g., Supporting). The tool will automatically calculate the "Commitment Gap".
  • Capability (Current vs. Target): Assess the stakeholder's current ability to adopt the change and what level of capability they will need. The "Capability Gap" is also calculated automatically.
  • Good Practice: Be as specific as possible. Instead of "IT Department", consider adding the "Head of IT" or "Network Manager" if they are the key individuals.

Once added, the main table provides powerful features:

  • Sorting & Filtering: Click any column header to sort by that column. Use the input boxes below the headers to filter the entire table in real-time.
  • Actions: You can Edit, Duplicate (which creates a quick copy), or Delete any stakeholder.
  • Conditional Formatting: Key columns like Gaps, Ratings, and Dates are colour-coded to provide at-a-glance insights.

Step 4: Create Your Action Plan (Engagement Plan Tab)

Analysis is only useful if it leads to action. Use the Engagement Plan tab to log and track your communications.

  • Add Engagement Log: When adding a log, you can select the specific stakeholder from a dropdown. This dropdown includes extra details (Group, Sub-Group, Location) to help you identify the correct person.
  • Track Progress: Set an owner, due date, and status (Planned, In Progress, Completed) for each activity.
  • Good Practice: Use this tab to plan future activities, not just to log past ones. This turns your analysis into a proactive engagement strategy.

Step 5: Visualise and Prioritise (Analysis Map & Dashboard)

  • Analysis Map: This provides a visual grid of your stakeholders based on their power, influence, interest, and impact. Use the dropdown filters at the top to narrow down the view and identify key groups. The legend on the right shows the colour-coding for each group.
  • Dashboard: This is your strategic overview. The interactive doughnut charts allow you to see the breakdown of your stakeholder landscape. Click on any segment of a chart to see a list of the stakeholders that make up that segment. From there, you can click a name to edit that stakeholder directly.

Good Practice Tips

  • Iterate and Review: Stakeholder analysis is a living process, not a one-off task. Revisit this tool regularly throughout your project lifecycle to update assessments as attitudes and situations change.
  • Collaborate: Use the Save to File and Load from File features to share your analysis with team members. Getting different perspectives will make your analysis more robust.
  • Focus on the Gaps: The "Commitment Gap" and "Capability Gap" are your primary indicators for action. A large gap means a significant engagement or training effort is required. Use the doughnut charts on the dashboard to identify where the biggest challenges lie.
